# Dermatology Patient No-Show Rates: Cosmetic vs. Medical Visits (2026)

URL: https://clinekthealth.com/blog/dermatology-patient-no-show-rates
Published: 2026-10-27

**Dermatology runs two different no-show problems under one schedule: a cosmetic calendar that patients pay for directly and usually keep, and a medical calendar, the skin check, the biopsy recheck, the mole-mapping visit, that drifts further out and gets missed far more often.** Published figures put the specialty's overall no-show rate near 10%, about double the rate for medical offices generally, and the gap between how reliably cosmetic patients show up and how often medical patients do not is wide enough that a single practice-wide average hides the real problem. A missed cosmetic slot is same-day lost revenue. A missed biopsy recheck is a care gap that can sit unnoticed until the next visit, whenever that is.

This guide covers what dermatology no-show rates actually look like once cosmetic and medical visits are split apart, why the gap exists, and how to run a backfill program that catches both kinds of missed visit the same day.

### Key Takeaways
- Dermatology's published no-show rate runs close to 10%, roughly double the rate reported for medical offices generally (Eastern, Dermatology News, 2019).
- Patients waited a median of 3.0 weeks for a cosmetic botulinum toxin visit versus 12.7 weeks for a routine, non-urgent skin exam and 9.0 weeks for an urgent mole check, all statistically significant gaps (Yadav et al., PLoS One, 2016).
- The longer the wait for a medical visit, the more time a patient has to feel fine, forget, or find another clinic, which is the single biggest driver behind the no-show gap.
- Two out of three melanoma-clinic patients, 67.1%, said they preferred to get a biopsy result by phone rather than a portal message, and the preference shifts again when the result is serious (Choudhry et al., JAMA Dermatology, 2015).
- Same-day backfill from a live list of patients overdue for a skin check or recall catches the slot a reminder alone cannot save.

## The Data Behind the Decision
The clearest single figure comes from a longtime Dermatology News practice-management column: the no-show rate in dermatology offices has averaged close to 10%, nearly double the rate for medical offices in general (Eastern, Dermatology News, 2019). For comparison, MGMA's aggregate no-show benchmark across all specialties was 6.81% in 2023, so dermatology's rate sits well above the cross-specialty average, not in line with it. The column names three drivers: a long gap between booking and the visit that gives a resolving symptom time to disappear, a no-show rate that climbs when the patient is paying out of pocket, and a weak relationship with the practice, which the column calls the single biggest factor.

The wait-time gap is measurable and it is specific to dermatology's mixed cosmetic and medical calendar. A population-wide survey of simulated scheduling calls found a median wait of 3.0 weeks for a cosmetic botulinum toxin visit, compared with 9.0 weeks for an urgent visit for a mole suspicious for melanoma and 12.7 weeks for a routine, non-urgent skin exam, with every difference statistically significant (Yadav et al., PLoS One, 2016). A patient booking and paying for a cosmetic visit waits a few weeks and shows up. A patient booked for a routine skin exam three months out has three months to forget why they booked it.

The biopsy recheck carries its own kind of avoidance. In a survey of 301 melanoma-clinic patients, 67.1% said they preferred to hear a biopsy result by phone rather than in person or through a portal message, and 40.5% said they would want a different channel if the result turned out to be malignant than the one they picked for a routine result (Choudhry et al., JAMA Dermatology, 2015). A patient who is anxious about a result is also a patient who may let the recheck visit slide rather than face it, and the channel a practice uses to re-offer that visit matters as much as the channel it used to deliver the result. Since 82% of patients try to book care outside a practice's regular office hours, a backfill program that only works the phones at 10 a.m. misses the moment most patients are actually trying to reschedule.

## Why do dermatology patients no-show?
Three patterns, and they call for different fixes. Cosmetic patients rarely no-show: they chose the visit, they are paying for it, and the wait is short. Medical patients, especially routine skin exams and follow-ups, no-show because the wait is long enough for the original reason to fade. Biopsy and excision rechecks no-show for a third reason entirely: the patient is avoiding a conversation they are anxious about, not forgetting an appointment they do not care about.

## How are no-shows different in a dermatology practice than in primary care?
A primary care no-show is usually one kind of visit on one kind of schedule. Dermatology runs two calendars that behave in opposite ways. The cosmetic calendar is high-margin and self-pay, and a no-show there is same-day revenue a practice cannot recover. The medical calendar carries the clinical risk: a missed annual skin check after a basal cell or melanoma diagnosis, a missed biopsy recheck, a missed isotretinoin lab visit. Practices running ModMed, Nextech, athenahealth, or eClinicalWorks see a canceled slot in the schedule the moment it happens. Almost none of them separate their no-show reporting by calendar, so a strong cosmetic show rate can mask a medical no-show problem that is quietly getting worse.

## What should a no-show program connect to in dermatology?
The EHR and the live schedule, so a cancellation or no-show is visible the instant it happens and the slot can be re-offered. A recall list that already tracks who is overdue for a skin check, a biopsy recheck, or an isotretinoin or biologic monitoring visit, so the same list that drives recall can fill a same-day gap. And the phone and text numbers patients already recognize, since a biopsy recheck offer from an unfamiliar number is easy to ignore.

## How to Reduce No-Shows in a Dermatology Practice: 6 Steps
1. **Split the report by calendar.** Measure no-shows separately for cosmetic, medical, and biopsy-recheck visits. A single blended rate hides which calendar is actually the problem.
2. **Shorten the wait on medical visits.** Hold same-day and next-day capacity for skin checks and follow-ups so the gap between booking and the visit does not stretch past 12 weeks.
3. **Offer the recheck conversation by phone first.** Patients told researchers they want a call for a biopsy result and for the follow-up that comes after it. A portal message alone will sit unread.
4. **Fix contact data at every visit.** Confirm the mobile number and preferred channel at check-in and in every outreach thread, not only at intake.
5. **Let patients choose their own time, including evenings.** A patient who picks a slot keeps it more often than one assigned a time during a call that caught them at work.
6. **Re-book every no-show the same day.** A missed biopsy recheck that is not rescheduled within a week is the start of a surveillance gap, not a one-time miss.

## What Should Still Go to a Human?
Whether a mole needs a biopsy or continued watching is a clinical decision, not a software one. Any patient who reports a new or changing lesion, a reaction, or distress about a result in a message thread should reach a nurse immediately, with the conversation attached. No-show fee policy for cosmetic visits, and how to handle a patient who repeatedly misses a medical recheck, belong to the practice manager and the physicians.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a normal no-show rate for a dermatology practice?**
Published figures put the specialty average near 10%, compared with a 6.81% cross-specialty benchmark from MGMA in 2023. Cosmetic visits run well below that; routine medical visits and follow-ups run above it, so the blended number depends heavily on the mix of visits a practice books.

**Why do cosmetic patients show up more reliably than medical patients?**
Mostly the wait. A population-wide study found a median 3.0-week wait for a cosmetic botulinum toxin visit versus 12.7 weeks for a routine skin exam. Cosmetic patients are also paying directly and chose the visit themselves, both of which track with lower no-show rates.

**Why do patients no-show for a biopsy recheck specifically?**
Anxiety about the result is a documented factor, not a guess. Two out of three melanoma-clinic patients said they preferred to get the result by phone, and a meaningful share wanted a different channel again if the news was serious. A recheck offer that arrives the same way, by phone or text from a number the patient recognizes, performs better than a single portal message.

**Do appointment reminders fix dermatology no-shows?**
They help with visits patients already intend to keep. They do not fill the slot once a patient cancels or never responds, and they do nothing for the skin check or biopsy recheck that was never booked in the first place. That is a recall problem.
