# What Is CJR-X? The Mandatory Joint Replacement Bundle, Explained for Orthopedic Leaders

**CJR-X is a proposed CMS payment model that would make roughly 2,500 U.S. hospitals financially accountable for the total cost and quality of every Medicare hip, knee, and ankle replacement across a 90 day episode of care, with a final rule expected in August 2026 and a proposed start date of October 1, 2027.** If your organization performs lower extremity joint replacement for Medicare patients, this model will likely shape your economics for years. Here is what the proposal says, who it affects, and why patient engagement sits at the center of it.

## Key Takeaways

- CJR-X is proposed as mandatory. Eligible hospitals do not opt in, and they cannot opt out.
- The model covers total knee, hip, and ankle replacement from the procedure through 90 days after surgery.
- Hospitals that beat their episode spending target can earn a reconciliation payment. Hospitals that exceed it repay CMS.
- A Composite Quality Score determines payment eligibility, and half of that score comes from patient experience surveys, patient reported outcomes, and hospital visits in the first week after surgery.
- Ambulatory surgery centers are excluded from the model, which will accelerate the shift of healthy patients to ASC settings.
- The final rule is expected in August 2026. Programs that build engagement infrastructure before go live will enter year one with their quality score already working.

## What is CJR-X?

CJR-X is the proposed successor to the Comprehensive Care for Joint Replacement (CJR) model, which ran from 2016 to 2024. Like its predecessor, it uses bundled payments: instead of paying separately for each service a joint replacement patient receives, CMS holds the hospital accountable for the total cost of the full episode.

The episode starts with the procedure and runs 90 days. Everything Medicare Part A and Part B pays for in that window counts toward the episode, with limited exclusions for unrelated care such as trauma or oncology.

Participation is mandatory for roughly 2,500 acute care hospitals nationwide, covering both inpatient and outpatient joint replacement. Excluded from the model: ambulatory surgery centers, hospitals already in the TEAM model, Maryland hospitals, facilities not paid under standard Medicare payment systems, and revision or bilateral procedures.

## How do hospitals get paid under CJR-X?

Hospitals continue to bill Medicare normally during the year. The accountability happens afterward, at reconciliation:

1. Before the year starts, CMS sets a target price for each episode type based on three years of regional spending data.
2. At year end, CMS compares the hospital's actual episode spending to its reconciled target.
3. If actual spending came in below target, the hospital receives a reconciliation payment.
4. If spending came in above target, the hospital repays CMS the difference.
5. Both payments and repayments are capped at 20 percent of the reconciliation target.

The proposal also allows gainsharing. Hospitals can share reconciliation payments with the surgeons who helped improve cost and quality, which gives independent orthopedic groups a direct financial stake in episode performance.

## What is the Composite Quality Score?

The Composite Quality Score (CQS) is a 0 to 20 point score that determines whether a hospital is eligible for a reconciliation payment at all, and how favorable its target price is. Hospitals scoring 6.0 or below are ineligible for reconciliation payments but still liable for repayments.

The score has three components:

- **Complications and early hospital visits, 50 percent.** Risk standardized complication rates through 90 days, plus unplanned admissions, observation stays, and emergency department visits within 7 days of surgery.
- **Patient experience, 40 percent.** HCAHPS and OAS CAHPS survey results covering communication, responsiveness, and discharge and recovery instructions.
- **Patient reported outcomes, 10 percent.** Measures of patient pain, function, and quality of life collected before surgery and through recovery.

Read that composition carefully. Half of the score is patient experience, patient reported outcome collection, and keeping patients out of the emergency department in the first week home. Those are not surgical variables. They are engagement variables.

## Why does patient engagement decide who wins?

Medicare claims analyses consistently show that 90 day episode costs are not evenly distributed. Most patients recover smoothly at modest cost. A small share of patients generate a large share of episode spending, driven by emergency visits, readmissions, and extended post acute care.

Those expensive episodes usually start the same way: a patient at home with a concern and no easy way to reach the care team. Swelling at 9pm on a Saturday becomes an emergency department visit. The ED visit becomes an observation stay. Under CJR-X, that chain of events damages the quality score and the episode spend at the same time.

The programs that win under bundled payment are the ones that stay connected to every patient across all 90 days: education before surgery, proactive check ins after, fast triage when something feels wrong, and consistent collection of patient reported outcomes. Recovery apps help the patients who enroll and stay active in them. The patients driving the costliest episodes are usually the ones who never engage with an app at all, which is why always available outreach across phone, text, and email matters.

## What should orthopedic leaders do before the final rule?

1. Confirm your exposure. If you operate or partner with an acute care hospital performing Medicare joint replacement, assume inclusion unless you fall under a stated exclusion.
2. Baseline your quality inputs. Pull your current HCAHPS results, PRO collection rates, and 7 day ED return rates for joint patients. These become your score.
3. Map your 90 day episode today. Where do your patients go after discharge, and who is talking to them between visits?
4. Build the engagement layer early. Outreach infrastructure, PRO collection, and escalation workflows take months to tune. Year one scores are set by what is running on day one.
5. For independent groups: understand gainsharing. Surgeons who can document their contribution to cost and quality improvement have a seat at the reconciliation table.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is CJR-X final?**
No. It is a proposed rule. The final rule is expected in August 2026, and program details may change. The proposed start date is October 1, 2027.

**Does CJR-X apply to ambulatory surgery centers?**
No. ASC procedures are excluded. Analysts expect this to accelerate migration of healthier patients to ASC settings, while hospitals retain the higher risk patients inside the bundle.

**Can surgeons share in the savings?**
Yes. The proposal allows hospitals to distribute reconciliation payments to providers who contribute to cost and quality improvements, and providers may also share repayment liability.

**What happens if a hospital's quality score is too low?**
Hospitals scoring 6.0 or below on the 20 point Composite Quality Score cannot receive reconciliation payments but remain liable for repayments. Quality is the gate to the upside.

**How is this different from the original CJR model?**
The original CJR model ran in selected metro areas from 2016 to 2024. The proposed CJR-X expands nationally to roughly 2,500 hospitals, adds total ankle replacement, includes outpatient procedures, and updates the quality methodology.
