# Patient Reactivation Software for Neurology Practices: What to Look for Before You Buy

**Neurology patient reactivation software has to catch three different dormant lists at once: epilepsy and MS patients who skip a scheduled monitoring visit, patients sitting on an EEG, EMG, or imaging order that was placed but never booked, and patients who had one specialist visit and never returned for a follow-up that should have happened months ago.** A generic recall tool built around a single days-since-last-visit trigger treats all three the same way, and that is why a neurology dormant list is often one of the largest sources of unworked demand a practice already has sitting in its own EHR.

### Key takeaways

- Neurology reactivation needs to segment by WHY a patient went dormant, not just days since last visit: a lapsed epilepsy or MS monitoring interval, an unscheduled EEG/EMG/imaging order, and a single-visit patient who never returned are three different problems with three different urgency levels.
- A prospective study at a national epilepsy clinic found 233 of 1,299 scheduled appointments (17.9%) were missed, and the single most common reason was simply forgetting the appointment.
- The same study found non-attendant patients carried a significantly higher rate of psychiatric comorbidity (41.6%, mainly depression, anxiety, and interictal psychosis) than patients who kept their appointments, meaning a neurology dormant list is itself a clinical risk signal, not just a scheduling inconvenience.
- The patients most likely to miss a neurology follow-up in the first place, younger patients, Medicaid/Medicare patients, and those with longer appointment lead times, are the same groups a one-size reminder sequence is least equipped to reach back.
- Reactivation is one of three places a neurology practice loses patients, alongside missed inbound calls and referrals that never convert to a first visit; software that only reactivates fixes one leak of three.

## Why does a dormant list matter more in neurology than the reminder pitch suggests?

A neurology dormant list is not a generic "patient who hasn't booked in a while" problem. A prospective study at the epilepsy clinic of Mexico's National Institute of Neurology and Neurosurgery tracked 1,299 scheduled appointments over ten months and found 233 (17.9%) were missed, with simply forgetting the appointment the leading cause, reported by 62 of the non-attendant patients (26.6%) (<a href="https://read.qxmd.com/read/34325158/increased-non-attendance-at-epilepsy-clinic-in-patients-with-neuropsychiatric-comorbidities-a-prospective-study" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Zertuche-Ortuño et al., Epilepsy &amp; Behavior, 2021</a>). The same study found non-attendees had a statistically significant higher rate of psychiatric comorbidity, 41.6% overall, mainly depression, anxiety, and interictal psychosis. That is the finding that matters for reactivation specifically: the patients who go dormant in a neurology panel are not a random cross-section, they skew toward a population that needs the follow-up more, not less.

A separate five-year review of 71,178 appointments across neurology clinics at the University of Arkansas for Medical Sciences found a 16% overall no-show rate, with higher odds tied to younger age, Medicaid or Medicare coverage, longer lead time between scheduling and the visit, and resident or stroke clinics specifically (<a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9025597/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Elkhider et al., Healthcare, 2022</a>). Longer lead times correlating with higher no-show risk is the same pattern that turns a single missed visit into a fully dormant patient: the longer a neurology practice waits to re-engage, the more likely that patient has already drifted out of care entirely. And 82% of patients try to book outside a practice's regular office hours, which is exactly when a lapsed patient is most likely to think about restarting and find nobody available to answer.

## What should neurology patient reactivation software actually cover?

Four things separate purpose-built neurology reactivation from a generic recall campaign tool.

<ul>
<li><strong>Dormancy-type segmentation.</strong> A patient overdue for a seizure-medication monitoring visit, a patient sitting on an EEG or imaging order that was placed but never scheduled, and a patient who had one consult and never returned are three different priorities. The software should route and script each differently, not run everyone through the same generic follow-up sequence.</li>
<li><strong>An EHR connection that matches your system.</strong> Epic, athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, NextGen, and Cerner all store order status and monitoring-interval data differently. The tool needs to read the real order and scheduling status in your specific system and put a booked visit directly back onto your existing schedule, not leave a list someone reconciles by hand.</li>
<li><strong>Comorbidity-aware escalation.</strong> Given how strongly missed visits correlate with psychiatric comorbidity in the evidence above, reactivation outreach needs to recognize when a patient's reply describes a new or worsening symptom and route it to clinical staff immediately, instead of treating every reply as a scheduling question.</li>
<li><strong>Consistency across subspecialty clinics.</strong> A neuroscience group running general neurology, epilepsy, MS, movement disorder, and stroke clinics side by side needs every subspecialty's dormant list worked to the same standard, since each carries a different monitoring cadence and a different risk profile if that cadence lapses.</li>
</ul>

## How do you evaluate neurology patient reactivation software before you buy?

<ol>
<li><strong>Pull your own dormant list first.</strong> Count patients by why they went dormant: a lapsed monitoring interval, an unscheduled EEG/EMG/imaging order, or a single visit with no return booking. The <a href="/leakage-calculator">patient leakage calculator</a> estimates what a list this size is worth to your practice in about two minutes.</li>
<li><strong>Ask how the tool segments by dormancy type.</strong> If the answer is everyone gets the same generic follow-up sequence regardless of whether they're overdue for an MS infusion check or just missed one general visit, that is a wellness-industry recall tool wearing a neurology label.</li>
<li><strong>Test a real scenario.</strong> Give the vendor an actual patient who is overdue for a monitoring visit and ask what happens if that patient's reply mentions a new or worsening symptom instead of just confirming a time.</li>
<li><strong>Confirm the tool books the appointment itself.</strong> Ask what happens the moment a lapsed patient replies yes. If the answer routes back to a staff queue, the tool has automated the outreach and left the highest-value step to your front desk.</li>
<li><strong>Check EHR order-status write-back for your specific system.</strong> Confirm the integration is live for Epic, athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, NextGen, or Cerner, whichever you run, and that it can see an EEG or imaging order's scheduled status, not just appointment history.</li>
<li><strong>For multi-subspecialty groups, ask for a clinic-by-clinic breakdown.</strong> Request dormant-list completion by subspecialty clinic, not a single aggregate number that can hide an underperforming epilepsy or MS clinic behind a healthy general neurology number.</li>
</ol>

## Does reactivation alone fix a neurology practice's patient leakage?

No, and a vendor that only sells reactivation will tell you it does anyway. An unworked dormant list is one of three places a neurology practice loses volume. The other two are missed inbound calls, especially the call after a new referral from primary care or the emergency department, and referrals that come in but never convert into a first scheduled visit. A practice that fixes reactivation while referral intake calls still go unanswered has closed one leak and left others open.
