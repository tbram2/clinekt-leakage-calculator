You reduce patient no-show rates by cutting the wait between booking and visit, sending reminders a patient can answer with one tap, letting patients cancel and rebook themselves at any hour, and refilling every opened slot the same day. Reminders alone plateau. The practices that stay under the MGMA median of 5% to 7% design their schedule so that missing an appointment is hard and moving one is easy. Key takeaways

  • The two strongest predictors of a missed appointment are a long wait between booking and visit and a prior no-show history (Dantas et al., Health Policy, 2018). Fix lead time first.
  • Automated reminders cut missed appointments from 23.1% to 17.3% in a study of 9,835 patients, and a live staff call cut them to 13.6% (Parikh et al., The American Journal of Medicine, 2010). Reminders work, and then they plateau.
  • The most cited peer-reviewed cost of a no-show is $196 per missed appointment, measured in 2008 dollars. A practice with 100 no-shows a month is losing roughly $19,600 of booked work, before counting downstream care.
  • Across Clinekt deployments, 82% of patients try to book outside office hours. A patient who cannot cancel or rebook at 9 pm often becomes tomorrow's silent no-show.
  • 42% of medical groups now charge a no-show fee (MGMA, 2025). The fee recovers a fraction of the loss and does nothing to refill the slot.

Why do patients miss appointments in the first place?

A systematic review of 105 studies found two predictors that beat everything else: how long the patient waited between booking and the visit, and whether they had missed before (Dantas et al., Health Policy, 2018). Cost was not the main driver. Clinics that charge patients nothing still average an 18.8% no-show rate.

Underneath both predictors sits friction. A patient whose week falls apart cannot reach your front desk at 9 pm to cancel, so the slot dies silently. A reminder that gets ignored is usually a reminder that demanded a phone call during business hours. Missed appointments cluster where the schedule asks patients to do inconvenient work to keep or move a visit.

Before you change anything, know your baseline. The MGMA median for US practices runs 5% to 7%, with single-specialty groups at 6.81% in 2023. Every benchmark, with its study, year, and setting, is in our guide to the average no-show rate and what each missed appointment costs.

What do no-shows actually cost your practice?

The most rigorous per-appointment figure is $196, measured across ten VA clinics (Kheirkhah et al., 2016). Those are 2008 dollars, so today's equivalent is higher.

Run your own version. A practice scheduling 1,400 appointments a month at a 7% no-show rate has 98 holes in the schedule. At $196 each, that is about $19,200 a month in provider time that was booked and then evaporated. For surgical specialties the figure understates the damage, because a no-showed new-patient consult is potentially a surgical case that books somewhere else.

How do you reduce patient no-show rates? Seven steps

  1. Cut the lead time for new patients. The longer the wait, the higher the miss rate, and it is the strongest predictor in the literature. Hold protected new-patient slots inside seven days, even if established follow-ups wait longer. The arithmetic: shortening new-patient waits lowers the risk on every appointment you book, all at once.
  2. Send reminders a patient can answer. One-way blasts confirm nothing. Use two-way messages with confirm, cancel, and reschedule as tap-level choices. In the Parikh study, patients with no reminder missed 23.1% of appointments and automated reminders cut that to 17.3%. On 1,400 monthly appointments, that difference is about 81 visits.
  3. Open cancellation and rebooking around the clock. 82% of patients try to book outside office hours, and patients strongly prefer handling their own appointments; the evidence is in our patient scheduling statistics. A cancellation with notice costs you nothing if the slot refills. A silent no-show costs $196.
  4. Treat referred patients as your highest-risk bookings. A referred new patient booked three weeks out carries both risk factors at once: long lead time and no relationship with your practice. Confirm them personally, and track them to a completed visit, because a referral that books and then no-shows is still a leaked referral. The numbers are in our referral leakage statistics.
  5. Keep a same-day backfill list. Maintain a standing list of patients who want to be seen sooner, and work it the moment a slot opens. Refilling just two slots a working day recovers about 42 appointments a month, roughly $8,200 at the per-appointment figure.
  6. Flag repeat no-shows and handle them differently. A prior miss is the second strongest predictor. Give repeat offenders shorter lead times, a personal confirmation call, or double-booked slots. One patient with three misses costs more than a dozen first-timers.
  7. Charge a fee last, not first. 42% of medical groups now charge a no-show fee (MGMA, 2025). A fee is fair once cancelling is genuinely easy, but it recovers a fraction of the $196 loss and refills nothing. Remove the friction before you add the penalty.

Do reminders still work when patients ignore them?

Yes, and the ceiling is real. Reminders move a practice from unmanaged to managed, then the remaining no-shows come from patients who saw the message and had no easy way to act on it. The controlled evidence, including studies showing 28.9% to 39.1% fewer missed visits from text reminders, is in do appointment reminders reduce no-shows. What happens after the reminder decides the outcome: a patient who taps cancel at 9 pm and rebooks themselves into Thursday is a kept appointment, and a patient who meant to call and never did is a hole in the schedule.

Common questions

What is the fastest way to reduce patient no-show rates?

Shorten the wait for new patients and switch to two-way reminders patients can act on. Lead time is the strongest predictor of a miss, and an answerable reminder converts silent no-shows into cancellations you can refill.

Do appointment reminder texts reduce no-shows?

Yes. Controlled studies show automated reminders cut missed appointments from 23.1% to 17.3%, and text reminders reduce missed visits by 28.9% to 39.1% against no reminder. The effect plateaus unless patients can also cancel and rebook easily.

Should you charge a no-show fee?

Only after cancelling and rebooking are genuinely easy. 42% of medical groups charge one, but a fee recovers only a fraction of the $196 average loss and does not refill the slot. Friction first, penalty second.

What is a good no-show rate for a medical practice?

MGMA data puts the median for US practices between 5% and 7%, with single-specialty groups at 6.81% in 2023. Under 5% is ahead of benchmark for a private practice. Compare against your own setting, not the global academic averages.

How do you fill a slot after a last-minute cancellation?

Keep a standing backfill list of patients who want earlier care, and contact them the moment a slot opens. Practices that automate this outreach routinely refill same-day openings that would otherwise sit empty.
