# Patient Recall Letter Template: 5 Examples You Can Use Today

**A patient recall letter template works when it names the specific reason a patient is due, leads with one clear next step, and has a second message already planned before the first one goes out.** Below are five templates you can copy today, what separates a recall letter that gets a reply from one that gets ignored, and why the template is rarely the part that decides whether the patient comes back.

Most practices treat the recall letter as the whole job. Write it once, mail it or text it, and move to the next patient on the list. The research on reminder and recall interventions says otherwise: the letter is the easy half.

## Key Takeaways

- A recall letter needs three things to work: the patient's name, the specific reason they're due, and one clear next step. Generic "we miss you" copy gets ignored.
- A [Cochrane Library systematic review](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD003941.pub3/full) (Jacobson Vann JC, et al., 2018) pooling 55 studies and 138,625 participants found reminder and recall interventions raised completed-visit rates by 8 percentage points over no intervention.
- A [BMC Ophthalmology study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2438329/) (Koshy, Car, Majeed, 2008) found non-attendance dropped from 18.1% with no reminder to 11.2% with a text reminder, a 38% relative reduction.
- Templates fix the wording. They don't fix the follow-up. Most recall failures happen because nobody sends a second message, not because the first one read poorly.
- Orthopedic, oral surgery, and endodontic practices need different templates than a six-month dental cleaning reminder. The reason for the visit changes what the letter should say.

## What should a patient recall letter include?

A recall letter that gets a response has four parts, in this order: the patient's name, the specific reason they're due, one way to respond, and a light deadline.

The order matters more than most practices assume. Patients skim mail and texts the same way they skim email: top to bottom, looking for the reason to keep reading. A letter that opens with the practice's history or a general wellness message loses the reader before it reaches the ask. Naming the exact reason ("you're due for your six-month cleaning" instead of "we haven't seen you in a while") gives the patient a reason to act instead of a reason to feel guilty and set the letter aside.

One way to respond beats three. A letter that offers to call, text, book online, or reply by mail hands the patient a decision to make before they can take the easy option. Pick the channel most of your patients already use and lead with that.

## What the research says about recall follow-through

A [Cochrane Library systematic review](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD003941.pub3/full) (Jacobson Vann JC, et al., "Patient reminder and recall interventions to improve immunization rates," Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2018) pooled 55 studies and 138,625 participants and found that reminder and recall interventions raised the share of patients completing the recommended visit by 8 percentage points on average, with telephone contact performing best among the channels studied. That number describes systems that kept trying, not single mailings.

A [BMC Ophthalmology observational study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2438329/) (Koshy, Car, Majeed, 2008) measured non-attendance at 18.1% among patients who received no reminder and 11.2% among patients who received a text reminder, a 38% relative reduction. The letter or text itself moved the number. But the same study only measured a single reminder attempt, which is the ceiling a one-shot recall letter can realistically reach.

Specialty care shows a sharper version of the same gap. A study of periodontal maintenance patients published in the [Journal of Indian Society of Periodontology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3110463/) (2010, 216 patients) found only 48.1% were compliant with their scheduled recall visits, even though most rated their care experience as good or excellent. The patients were not dissatisfied. Nobody reached them in a way that turned into a booked visit.

## How is a recall letter different from a reactivation letter?

Recall reaches a patient who is due for a follow-up but hasn't booked it yet. Reactivation reaches a patient who has gone fully dormant, with no visit on the calendar and no plan for one. The wording changes because the relationship changes: a recall letter can assume recent contact and a known next step, while a reactivation letter has to re-earn attention from a patient who may not remember the practice reached out at all. ## How to write a patient recall letter that gets a response

1. **Name the exact reason, not "your appointment."** "You're due for your six-month cleaning" gets more replies than "we haven't seen you in a while."
2. **Put the next step first, not last.** If the phone number or booking link is buried in the third paragraph, most readers never reach it.
3. **Give one action, not three.** A letter offering to call, text, book online, or reply by mail leaves the patient a decision to make before the easy one.
4. **Set a light deadline.** "Before the end of the month" moves people who would otherwise let a recall letter sit indefinitely.
5. **Plan the second message before you send the first.** The Cochrane review's strongest results came from repeated contact, not a single mailing.
6. **Track who responded and remove them from the next round.** Sending the same recall letter to a patient who already booked reads as careless, not thorough.

## 5 patient recall letter templates you can use today

Copy any of these and adjust the bracketed fields. Adjust the register as well as the wording: an oral surgery post-op check and a routine dental hygiene recall should not read the same way.

**1. Standard recall (due for a routine visit)**

> Dear [Patient Name],
>
> Our records show it has been [interval, e.g. six months] since your last visit with [Provider Name] at [Practice Name]. Regular [visit type] appointments help catch small issues before they become bigger ones.
>
> Please call us at [Phone Number] or book online at [Booking Link] before [date] to schedule your next visit.
>
> We look forward to seeing you again.
>
> [Practice Name]

**2. Post-procedure follow-up recall (orthopedic, oral surgery)**

> Dear [Patient Name],
>
> It's time for your follow-up visit after your [procedure type] with [Provider Name] on [procedure date]. This visit lets us confirm your recovery is on track and address anything that has come up since your procedure.
>
> Call [Phone Number] to schedule, or reply to this message with a few times that work for you.
>
> [Practice Name]

**3. Imaging or treatment-course recall**

> Dear [Patient Name],
>
> Your treatment plan with [Provider Name] calls for a follow-up [imaging type / visit type] around this time. Staying on schedule gives your provider the clearest picture of how your treatment is progressing.
>
> Please call [Phone Number] or book at [Booking Link] within the next two weeks.
>
> [Practice Name]

**4. Second-attempt recall (no response to the first message)**

> Dear [Patient Name],
>
> We reached out on [date] about scheduling your [visit type] and haven't heard back. We don't want you to lose your place on [Provider Name]'s schedule.
>
> If now isn't a good time, reply and let us know what works better. Otherwise, call [Phone Number] and we'll find a time this week.
>
> [Practice Name]

**5. Short recall text message**

> Hi [Patient First Name], it's [Practice Name]. You're due for your [visit type] with [Provider Name]. Reply YES to book, or call [Phone Number]. Reply STOP to opt out.

## What's different about recall letters for orthopedic, oral surgery, and endodontic practices?

Most recall letter templates online are written for the dental hygiene use case: a cleaning every six months on a predictable interval. Orthopedic, oral surgery, and endodontic practices don't run that schedule, and using hygiene-recall wording on their patients misses most of who actually needs a letter.

For an orthopedic practice, the reason for the visit is a post-op check, an imaging follow-up, or the next step in a treatment course, which template 2 and template 3 above are built for. For oral surgery and endodontics, the same post-op logic applies, with a referring general dentist often expecting to hear that the patient was seen, which is a separate conversation covered in [Referral Management Software](https://clinekthealth.com/blog/referral-management-software). The fix is not a different letter format. It's naming the real reason the patient is due, instead of borrowing hygiene-recall language that doesn't describe their treatment.

## Does a template alone fix recall, or does something have to run it?

A template gives staff the words. It doesn't run the list, send the second message on schedule, or notice that a patient who ignored two letters would answer a text. That's the part that quietly drops off once the first mailing goes out, because reaching every name more than once takes staff time nobody has budgeted for.

At Baldwin Bone & Joint, an orthopedic group, Clinekt's Recall agent worked the overdue and dormant list on a standing cadence instead of a single mailing, and the practice's patient activation system produced 263 qualified surgical leads and 159 booked appointments in a single quarter, a 60% booking rate. The templates above cover the first attempt well. What decided the outcome was whether a second and third attempt happened at all, on a channel the patient actually answers.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a patient recall letter?

A message, sent by mail, email, or text, that reminds a patient who is due for a follow-up visit to schedule it. It differs from an appointment reminder, which confirms a visit that's already booked.

### How often should I send a patient recall letter?

Send the first message when the patient becomes due, then a second attempt within two to three weeks if there's no response. The Cochrane review's pooled data found repeated contact attempts outperformed a single mailing.

### Should I use a letter, text, or email for patient recall?

Use more than one. The BMC Ophthalmology study found text reminders cut non-attendance by more than a third compared with no reminder at all. Letters still work for patients who prefer mail, but they shouldn't be the only channel a practice relies on.

### What's the difference between a recall letter and a reactivation letter?

A recall letter reaches a patient who is due for care but hasn't booked yet. A reactivation letter reaches a patient who has gone fully dormant. [Reminders vs Recall vs Reactivation](https://clinekthealth.com/blog/reminders-vs-recall-vs-reactivation) covers the difference in full.

### Do these templates work for orthopedic and oral surgery practices?

Yes, once the visit reason is changed. Replace the hygiene-interval language with the actual reason the patient is due: a post-op check, an imaging follow-up, or the next step in a treatment course.
